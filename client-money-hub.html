How strictly the money is regulated, and how risky the flows are, predict what a firm needs better than its size does.

Two axes

Tier 1 is a prescriptive rulebook with the regulator supervising the money. Tier 2 is professional-body rules plus accountant reporting. Tier 3 has statutory duties but no supervisor of the money itself. Tier 4 has no specific client money regime. Banks often call Tier 3 “unregulated”, which is inaccurate.

The common ideal journey

Every segment needs the same eight stages, with different emphasis.

  2. Apply oncePre-filled from Companies House and the regulator's register.
  3. VerifyA named contact, a status tracker and a target in days, not weeks.

What payment preferences mean for a bank

Users want to pay from the system they already work in, with approvals and payee checks built in, and see each payment matched to their ledger straight away.

1 · Initiate from their software

Practice, property or back-office systems submit payments by API or file, with the bank's approval rules applied.

2 · Approval rules by value and role

For example, one approver below £10k and two above, with separate rules for client-to-office transfers. SRA rules require a bill or written notice before costs move to the office account.

3 · Payee checks before money leaves

Confirmation of Payee plus account verification for high-value completions.

4 · Inbound matching

A virtual account or unique reference per matter, tenancy or client removes most manual reconciliation. Pay-by-bank deposits cut errors and fraud.

5 · Trust-money guardrails

Block overdrafts, card spending and unapproved direct debits on pooled accounts. Allow them only on designated accounts where the regime permits, such as deputyships.

6 · Confirmation and tracking

An instant status and reference for every payment, fed back to the customer's ledger, especially completion-day CHAPS.

Regulation and account types

Regulation will reshape this market more than product design over 2026–2027.

Strictly, CASS covers FCA firms only. Law firms follow the SRA or CLC rules, accountants follow ICAEW or ACCA, and letting agents follow CMP scheme rules. The proposition treats “CASS-grade” as the common standard.

FSCS and client money

  • The limit is £120,000 per person per banking licence. Temporary high balance cover is £1.4m for six months.
  • Cover looks through to the beneficial owner. A client's share of a pooled account is added to their own deposits at the same banking licence.
  • Payouts can take up to three months where beneficial ownership isn't obvious.
  • Large companies are generally ineligible.
  • Firms need client consent to share details with the FSCS.
  • E-money and payment firms' customer funds have no FSCS protection. The FCA reported average shortfalls of 65% in payment firms that failed between 2018 and 2023.
OpportunityBanks that can produce FSCS-ready beneficiary data for client accounts, and help firms spread large balances across banking licences, have a real selling point.

Case study: Axiom Ince

The main reason the SRA is tightening client money oversight.

What happened

  • Apr–Jul 2023: Axiom DWFM buys Ince Gordon Dadds, then Plexus Legal, out of administration.
  • Jul 2023: a large client account shortfall is found after an SRA visit.
  • 3 Oct 2023: the SRA intervenes and closes the firm. About 1,400 jobs affected and about £64m of client money missing.
  • Dec 2024: the SFO charges five men, including the CEO and CFO.
  • Late 2024: an LSB-commissioned review finds the SRA's handling inadequate. Failing to ringfence the account after July 2023 allowed about £36m more to be lost.
  • 15 Feb 2027: criminal trial due to start. The charges are unproven.

Why it matters for a bank

  • It sits directly behind the SRA's 2027 reforms: mandatory report submission, separate compliance roles, and scrutiny of acquisitive firms.
  • It fuels the TPMA debate: if firms don't hold client money, it can't be misused.
  • Banks see client account flows in real time. Alerts on unusual outflows, and balance confirmations sent directly to the reporting accountant or regulator, are where a bank can add protection.
  • The Compensation Fund had paid £37.2m in grants by October 2025. Nothing in the public record suggests the banks involved were at fault.

Six commitments behind the end state

Trust by design

Right structure, letters on day one

The right account structure for the regime, with acknowledgement letters issued in the prescribed wording at account opening. Regulatory registers are checked automatically at onboarding and monitored afterwards.

Embedded

Inside the customer's software

Accounts, ledgers and payments are available by API and events, so firms use them from their practice, property or platform software. Integrations with the main vendors in each sector are certified.

One truth

Continuous reconciliation

Bank and vendor ledgers reconcile continuously. Audit, accountant and regulator evidence is generated from the same data.

Controlled movement

Money moves only when it should

Payee checks, approval rules by value and role, and release on events from the customer's software. Trust-money guardrails block overdrafts and set-off.

Fair-value interest

Published and passed through

Published tiered rates, per-client pass-through and multi-bank options. Interest rules are designed so they can adapt to the outcome of the ILCA levy.

Access and resilience

Open fast, stay open

Published eligibility, onboarding measured in days, and risk-based pooled accounts through platform partners. Exit notice periods match realistic re-banking times. Resolution-pack and FSCS data are available on request.
